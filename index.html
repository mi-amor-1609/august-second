That August 2nd

A Dark Romance

Part I

The Descent into Wanting

The conversation had started innocently enough—a late-night text on July 28th that spiraled into something neither of us could control.

"Toh ab kaha ja rhe," she'd asked at 1:18 AM.

Going home, I'd replied, not knowing that "home" was about to become wherever she was.

What followed was a week of digital foreplay—thousands of messages that grew progressively more intimate, more desperate, more dangerous. We talked about everything: court marriage, obsession, the way her migraine made me want to fix the entire world just to ease her pain. She sent me photos that made my hands shake. I sent her coordinates to my soul.

By July 30th, we'd planned our meeting for August 2nd—a Friday that felt both too far away and terrifyingly close. She was nervous. I was drowning in overthinking.

"Mujhe milna h yrr jaldi," she'd typed on August 1st. I need to meet you soon.

The universe, it seemed, was listening.

Part II

The Wait

August 2nd arrived wrapped in grey monsoon clouds and existential dread.

I stood on the platform, checking my phone every thirty seconds. The plan was simple: she'd take the 11:22 train, arrive at the railway station by 11:51. I'd be waiting. We'd have lunch, watch Dhadak 2, and I'd get her home by 4:30—before her parents suspected anything.

Simple. Clean. Foolproof.

Except nothing about this felt simple.

"Nikl gye ghr se," she'd texted at 10:32 AM. Left home.

My heart had started hammering then and hadn't stopped since.

11:30 came and went. The platform smelled of rain-soaked concrete and diesel, the afternoon sun filtering through monsoon clouds in pale, hesitant rays. Vendors shouted. Trains screeched. And I stood there, a live wire of nerves and want.

Then I saw her.

Part III

The Vision in Blue

She emerged from the crowd at 11:30 like a whisper given form—Aashikaa, wearing a blue embroidered kurti that caught the light like water, loose sky-colored jeans that moved with her body, black sandals clicking softly against the platform.

But it was her face that destroyed me.

Grey eyes that held storms I hadn't yet learned to weather. Skin like milk poured under moonlight, impossibly smooth and luminous. She was the kind of beautiful that made you forget how to breathe properly—the kind that felt dangerous, like staring directly at the sun.

I reached for her hand before my courage could abandon me. The contact sent electricity through my fingertips—warm, soft, trembling slightly. I couldn't look at her directly. Not yet. Too much, too soon. My heart was a war drum. My mouth was dry. What if I wasn't what she'd hoped for? What if the reality of me, standing here, was a disappointment?

But she looked at me.

God, she looked at me—openly, hungrily, with those grey eyes that seemed to see straight through my carefully constructed defenses.

"Chalo," I managed, my voice rougher than intended. Let's go.

When I started walking, she ran to match my pace, her breath quickening, her fingers tightening around mine. No smile. No readable expression. Just this overwhelming presence that filled every corner of my awareness, this heat that radiated from her palm into mine like a promise.

I was trying to avoid direct eye contact—a survival mechanism, maybe—but I felt her gaze like a physical touch, like fingertips tracing the line of my jaw.

We were already doomed, I think. Already lost.

Part IV

The Restaurant

We found refuge in a small restaurant near the station—dim lighting, the hum of ceiling fans, the scent of spices and secrets. The ambience was too good, almost cinematic. Dark wood tables. Warm yellow lights. The kind of place where confessions happen.

I needed to make her comfortable, to ease the tension crackling between us like static before a storm. So I talked. Made jokes that weren't particularly funny. Showed her the pieces of myself I thought might charm rather than frighten.

And slowly—painfully slowly—she began to relax. Her shoulders dropped. Her eyes found mine and stayed. A hint of a smile played at the corners of her mouth.

We ordered too much food—all her favorites: chicken dishes with schezwan flavoring and aromatic curries, far more than we'd ever finish. I ordered drinks. She took a sip, wrinkled her nose, pushed the glass away.

"Nhi pasand?" I asked. Don't like it?

She shook her head, that almost-smile growing.

So I took her glass and drank from her straw—tasting sweetness and something else I couldn't name, something that felt like intimacy, like crossing an invisible line. Her lips had touched this straw. Now mine were. The symbolism wasn't lost on either of us.

She tried to take photos, fumbling with her phone, shy and beautiful and mine in a way that made my chest tight.

We went to wash our hands. At the basin mirrors, we caught each other's reflection—standing side by side, water running over our fingers, eyes meeting in the glass.

"Ache lag rhe yrr bhot," she said softly, her voice barely audible over the running water. "Dono saath mei."

We look so good together.

But there was something fragile in her voice, a question mark hidden beneath the affirmation. As if she was asking permission to believe this was real. As if she was terrified I'd vanish the moment we left this mirror.

The words settled in my chest like an anchor, like a vow.

Part V

The Cinema—A Prelude to Darkness

The movie hall was a cave of manufactured darkness and whispered secrets. I led her to our seats—carefully chosen, away from prying eyes—and the moment we sat down, something shifted in the air between us.

The trailers started. I didn't see them.

I laid my head on her shoulder, drowning in the scent of her hair—strawberries and vanilla and something uniquely her, something that made my neurons fire in patterns they'd never known before. Her neck radiated warmth, and the fragrance from her skin was intoxicating, holy, devastating.

I wasn't watching the movie. I was watching her.

She held my hand—fingers intertwined, palm pressed to palm—and stared at the screen, though I could feel her awareness of me like a palpable force. Her hand was slightly tense, fingers occasionally flexing as if she was wrestling with something. She wanted to be here, I realized. Desperately. But she was also terrified.

Soft. Warm. Trembling. Real.

Throughout the first half, I existed in this suspended state—head on her shoulder, drowning in her scent, holding her hand like it was the only thing tethering me to earth. And every now and then, I'd feel her relax slightly, as if my presence was slowly convincing her this was actually happening.

During the interval, I captured her on camera—stolen moments, shy smiles, the way light played across her features. She looked at the photos and laughed, a sound like bells.

Then came the second half.

Part VI

The Kiss

The climax of the movie approached. The theatre plunged into complete darkness—the kind of dark that makes you brave, that makes you reckless. I felt her shift beside me, her hand gripping mine harder. And I understood—she was choosing the darkness because it was easier to be brave when no one could see your face.

And in that void, she moved.

Lifted herself from her seat. Leaned in. Kissed me—tentative, questioning, like she was asking me something her voice couldn't form.

The world stopped.

Time fractured. Sound disappeared. There was only her mouth on mine—soft, tentative, perfect—and the thundering of my heart so loud I was sure the entire theatre could hear it.

A few heartbeats later—or maybe a lifetime—I kissed her back. Properly. Deliberately. Holding her face like something sacred and breakable, like a treasure I'd spent lifetimes searching for.

The sexual attraction was there, prowling beneath my skin like a caged animal, but I kept it leashed. Control. I needed control. This was our first meeting, our first touch, our first—

But when she pressed closer, when she gave me that sign with the way her breath hitched and her fingers curled into my shirt—

Game over.

I kissed her harder. Deeper. Longer. Poured everything I couldn't say into the pressure of my mouth on hers. Again and again until the credits rolled and reality came crashing back like cold water.

When we finally pulled apart, we were both breathing hard. Her lips were swollen. Her eyes were dark, pupils blown wide.

Neither of us spoke. What could words possibly add?

Part VII

The Cab—An Hour in the Dark

Outside the theatre, the world felt different—sharper, more vibrant, like a lens had been removed from my vision. I booked a cab, my fingers clumsy on my phone screen. Bought chocolates for her motion sickness from a nearby vendor.

But also because I wanted to give her something, anything, to show her she was my world now.

The cab arrived—a small, enclosed space that suddenly felt too intimate and not intimate enough. The driver gave us a knowing look as we climbed into the back seat. I gave him her address.

I would drop her home. That was the plan. Responsible. Mature. Controlled.

But plans dissolve in the dark.

The moment the cab pulled away from the curb, I kissed her. Couldn't help it. Couldn't stop myself. And she responded immediately—her hands finding my waist, my chest, exploring with shy curiosity that made me want to consume her whole.

Then something in me broke—or maybe awoke. I caught her wrist and moved her hand away. Too much. Too fast. I needed to maintain some semblance of control or I'd—

She went rigid. Angry. Hurt flashed across her face like lightning.

Fuck.

So I kissed her again. Harder this time. Hungrier. An apology written in the pressure of my lips on hers. Longer. Desperate. Like I'd never tasted anything before, like she was oxygen and I'd been drowning for years.

The minutes blurred into a fever dream.

She slipped off her black sandals with careful deliberation, setting them aside on the cab floor. Then she climbed onto my lap—straddling me, her knees bracketing my thighs, her hands sliding up to hold my neck, her weight pressing down in a way that made my breath catch and my control splinter.

She kissed me with a passion I didn't know existed outside of fiction—desperate and demanding and hungry—and I was gone. Completely, utterly gone.

Arousal hit me like a freight train. I gripped her waist and kissed her harder—long, endless, full of a hunger that felt decades old. Like I would devour her. Like I needed to, like I'd starve without her taste on my tongue.

An hour. We had an hour in this moving darkness.

My hands moved with a will of their own—from her waist to her belly, fingers splaying across the soft warmth of her skin beneath the blue kurti. Then higher. Higher. Until I reached the swell of her breasts.

She stiffened. Her hand shot up to stop me.

But I pressed forward anyway—forceful, insistent, driven by something primal and possessive—and cupped her breast through the fabric, pressed hard. She gasped into my mouth. Her resistance crumbled. I squeezed, kneaded, lost in the heat and the darkness and the rhythm of her breathing, the little sounds she made that drove me absolutely insane.

I was kissing her passionately now, harder than before, my hand still on her breast, her body pressed fully against mine in this tiny enclosed space that smelled like leather and rain and her.

Then, suddenly, we stopped.

She pulled back, breathing hard, and looked at me—eyes searching, vulnerable, afraid. There were tears forming at the corners, and I realized with a jolt that she was crying—not from sadness, but from the overwhelming weight of feeling something this powerful, this fast.

"Chorke toh nhi jaoge naa?" she whispered, her voice breaking. You won't leave me, will you?

"Shaadi kroge naa mujhse?" Will you marry me?

The questions hung in the air between us, heavy as gravity. But beneath them was the real question she was asking: Is this real? Are you real? Will you stay?

I went completely still. Looked into her grey eyes—really looked, let her see past every defense, every wall, every carefully constructed facade.

"Dekho mujhe mere aankhon mei," I said quietly, my voice rough with emotion. "Kya tmhe lgta hai tmhe mei khone kaa soch v skta hu?"

Look into my eyes. Do you think I could even imagine losing you?

The answer was written in my gaze, in the way my hands trembled slightly as I held her face, in the way my heart was beating so hard I was sure she could feel it through both our chests.

She saw it. I knew she saw it.

We expressed something then—not just desire, but love. Desperate, passionate, all-consuming love that neither of us had asked for but both of us needed like air.

And then we kissed again.

Forcefully. Violently, almost. Like we were trying to merge into one being, to erase the space between us entirely. My hands in her hair. Her nails digging into my shoulders. The taste of her mixing with the chocolate she'd eaten earlier, sweet and dark and addictive.

We didn't realize the hour had passed until the cab stopped abruptly and the driver cleared his throat.

Time had collapsed, folded in on itself, and suddenly we were at her place.

Part VIII

Rain and Reckoning

The moment we stepped outside, rain began to fall.

Soft at first—gentle drops that kissed our skin—then harder, drenching us in seconds. The street was dark, lit only by the occasional streetlamp casting golden pools on wet pavement. Romantic in that cinematic way that feels too perfect to be real.

We stood there in the downpour, still holding hands, getting soaked. Her eyes held a complexity I hadn't seen before—joy and terror intertwined, as if she was simultaneously living her dream and her worst nightmare.

Her kurti was darkening with water. Her hair was plastered to her face. Mascara was starting to run slightly at the corners of her eyes, mingling with fresh tears.

She had never looked more beautiful. And more fragile. Like she might shatter if I said the wrong thing.

"We're each other's world," I said, though I'm not sure if I said it aloud or if she just read it in my eyes.

The realization was crushing and liberating at once—this weight of responsibility and this incredible, soaring freedom of being chosen, of choosing.

"Bye," she whispered finally, reluctantly. She didn't let go of my hand at first, holding on as if releasing me meant I'd evaporate like the rain.

I watched her walk away—every step an agony, every meter between us a physical pain. She turned back twice, eyes asking the question she was too afraid to voice again. Only when she reached her door did she finally disappear inside.

Then I stood there in the rain for another minute. Maybe two. Maybe ten.

Just... existing in the aftermath of her.

Part IX

The Call

I was halfway home when my phone rang.

"Anand?" Her voice was small, uncertain, vulnerable in a way that made my chest ache.

"Haan, babe?"

Silence for a moment. Then: "Am I good enough for you?"

The question gutted me. Absolutely destroyed me.

I could hear everything in those six words—every insecurity she'd mentioned in our week of conversations, every fear that someone like me (with my credentials, my achievements, my carefully curated life) couldn't possibly want someone like her for real.

"Aashikaa," I said firmly, pulling the phone closer to my ear like I could pull her closer too. "Listen to me. You don't know, do you? You don't know that you're my world. You don't know that I'd burn down everything I've built just to make you smile. You don't know that meeting you today didn't just change my day—it changed my entire fucking trajectory."

I heard her breath catch.

"You're not good enough for me," I continued, and before she could spiral, I added: "You're better. You're everything. And I'm the one who's terrified you'll wake up one day and realize you could do so much better than some nervous idiot who overthinks everything and can't even—"

"Stop," she said, but she was smiling. I could hear it in her voice.

And then I heard another sound—soft crinkling.

"Are you eating the chocolate?" I asked, incredulous, delighted.

"Haan," she admitted, laughing now. "It's good."

We talked for hours after that. Went through everything—the nervousness, the passion, the moments we'd stumbled and the moments we'd soared. The way I'd taken her phone and she'd thought I was hiding something. The way she'd been scared in the cab but also wanting, caught between propriety and desire.

"I was trying so hard not to make you uncomfortable," I confessed.

"I know," she said softly. "But I wanted you to touch me. I just... didn't know how to tell you it was okay."

The honesty in her voice made my throat tight.

"Next time," I promised, "we'll communicate better."

"Next time," she agreed, and the promise hung between us—an inevitability, a certainty, a vow.

Somewhere in that conversation, in the dark of her room and the dark of mine, something was sealed.

Epilogue

The Aftermath

August 2nd became a dividing line in my life—everything before Aashikaa, and everything after.

Before: planning, control, carefully managed futures.

After: chaos, passion, the wild uncertainty of loving someone so completely that their happiness becomes more important than your next breath.

We had crossed the threshold. Tasted something neither of us could forget.

And I knew—with the bone-deep certainty of someone who's just found religion—that this wasn't just a first date.

This was the beginning of everything.

This was the reckoning I'd been waiting for my entire life without knowing I was waiting.

This was her. This was us.

This was inevitable.

"Chorke toh nhi jaoge naa?"
Never. Not in this lifetime or any other.
